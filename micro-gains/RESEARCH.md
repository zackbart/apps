# Micro Gains research notes

Compiled 2026-08-19. Every number below comes from a cited primary source or a named
systematic review. Where the evidence is thin I say so.

## 1. Summary of the evidence

### What is well supported

**Very short bouts of vigorous activity track with lower mortality and cancer risk.**
Stamatakis et al. (Nature Medicine, 2022) analysed 25,241 UK Biobank adults who reported
no leisure exercise, wrist accelerometry, 6.9 years follow-up, 852 deaths. Median vigorous
intermittent lifestyle physical activity (VILPA) was 4.4 min/day accrued in roughly 3
bouts. At that median: all-cause mortality HR 0.73 (95% CI 0.63-0.85), CVD 0.66 (0.50-0.88),
cancer 0.70 (0.59-0.84). The minimum dose producing half the maximum benefit was 3.4 min/day
or 1.4-1.5 bouts/day. The dose-response was near-linear with no clear plateau. Bouts were
defined as up to 1 min and up to 2 min at >=64% VO2max.

The companion cancer-incidence paper (Stamatakis et al., JAMA Oncology, 2023; n = 22,398,
2,356 cancers) found 92.3% of all VILPA happened in bouts of **under one minute**. At the
median 4.5 min/day, total cancer HR 0.80 (0.69-0.92) and physical-activity-related cancer
HR 0.69 (0.55-0.86). Minimum dose 3.4-3.7 min/day.

Caveat: both are observational. Reverse causation and residual confounding are live risks
in a cohort where the exposure is "can you run for a bus".

**Stair-climbing exercise snacks raise VO2peak in sedentary people.**
Jenkins et al. (Appl Physiol Nutr Metab, 2019): sedentary young adults, 3 bouts/day of
vigorously climbing 60 stairs, separated by 1-4 h of recovery, 3 days/week for 6 weeks
(n = 12 per group). VO2peak higher in climbers (p = 0.003), though the authors call the
absolute increase "modest".

A larger 2024 RCT (n = 42, 21.6 +/- 2.3 yr) used 3 x 30 s all-out stair climbs (126 steps,
18.9 m), >1 h apart, 3 days/week for 6 weeks against 40 min of 60-70% HRmax cycling.
Exercise snacks raised VO2peak 7% (MD 2.5 mL/kg/min, 95% CI 1.2-3.7, d = 0.44); the cycling
group changed 1.0 mL/kg/min (95% CI -1.1 to 3.2, non-significant); control fell
1.7 mL/kg/min. Maximal fat oxidation did not move in any group.

**Breaking up sitting acutely improves postprandial glucose and insulin.**
Dunstan et al. (Diabetes Care, 2012), 19 overweight/obese adults aged 45-65, three-period
crossover: 2 min of walking every 20 min cut glucose iAUC from 6.9 to 5.2 (light) and 4.9
(moderate) mmol/L.h and insulin iAUC from 828.6 to ~634 pmol/L.h (p < 0.0001).

Quan et al. (J Sport Health Sci, 2021), network meta-analysis of 30 crossover RCTs and 9
interruption modes: light-intensity intermittent breaks lowered postprandial glycaemia
SMD -0.46 (95% CI -0.70 to -0.21) and moderate-intensity SMD -0.69 (-1.00 to -0.37).
Moderate intensity ranked best.

Duran et al. (Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2023) is the only proper frequency x duration
dose-response trial. Eleven middle-aged and older adults, 8 h sitting, five conditions.
Only **5 min of walking every 30 min** significantly cut glucose iAUC (-11.8, p = 0.017).
**Every dose lowered systolic BP**, with the largest drops from 1 min every 60 min
(-5.2 mmHg) and 5 min every 30 min (-4.3 mmHg). So the cheap dose buys the blood pressure
effect; the expensive dose is needed for glucose.

Bodyweight movement works here too, not just walking. Moore et al. (J Appl Physiol, 2022),
12 adults, 7.5 h trials: 15 bodyweight sit-to-stands every 30 min raised myofibrillar
protein synthesis to 0.103 %/h vs 0.080 %/h sitting (walking 0.118 %/h), and squats were
the only condition to raise rpS6 phosphorylation (7.6-fold vs 1.6-fold). A separate 7.5 h
trial (J Appl Physiol, 2021, n = 14) with 15 chair stands every 30 min cut 1 h post-lunch
insulin iAUC 37% and 3 h iAUC 24% versus uninterrupted sitting; the 2 min walk condition
cut 1 h iAUC 29% but the 3 h effect was not significant.

**High-frequency, low-volume, non-failure training builds strength.**
Sato/Yoshida/Nosaka's series is the cleanest minimal-dose evidence in existence. One 3-second
maximal eccentric elbow-flexor contraction per day, 5 days/week for 4 weeks raised MVC
torque >10% (12.8 +/- 9.6% concentric, 12.2 +/- 7.8% eccentric). Dropping to 3 days/week gave
much smaller gains (2.5% and 3.9%); 2 days/week gave nothing (Yoshida et al., Eur J Appl
Physiol, 2024). Frequency, not session size, carried the effect.

Grgic et al. (Sports Med, 2018), 22 studies: strength effect sizes rose 0.74 -> 0.82 -> 0.93
-> 1.08 for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ sessions/week, but the effect vanished when volume was equated
(p = 0.421). Read that carefully. Frequency helps mainly because it is a way to accumulate
volume, not because frequency is magic.

**Training to failure is not required.** The 2026 ACSM position stand (Currier et al., Med
Sci Sports Exerc, 58(4):851-872), an overview of 137 systematic reviews and >30,000
participants, concluded that "training to momentary muscle fatigue, equipment type, exercise
complexity, set structure, time under tension... did not consistently impact training
outcomes", and that bodyweight and home-based routines produce real benefit. Robinson et al.
(Sports Med, 2024) meta-regressed proximity to failure and found the slope for **strength**
across repetitions-in-reserve was statistically indistinguishable from zero, while
hypertrophy did improve with closer proximity to failure. For an app whose goal is fitness
and adherence rather than maximum muscle size, stopping several reps short of failure costs
close to nothing.

**Small home protocols move real function in older adults.**
Perkin et al. (J Aging Res, 2019), 28-day unsupervised home "exercise snacking" twice daily,
n = 10 vs 10: 60 s sit-to-stand score up 31% (p < 0.01), leg press power +6%, thigh CSA +2%.

**Habits need consistency and simplicity, not intensity.**
Lally et al. (Eur J Soc Psychol, 2010): median 66 days to asymptotic automaticity, range 18
to 254 days, with the behaviour anchored to a daily cue. Kaushal and Rhodes (J Behav Med,
2015), 111 new gym members over 12 weeks: **at least 4 bouts/week for 6 weeks** was the
minimum to establish exercise habit, and low behavioural complexity (beta = 0.19) and
consistency (beta = 0.21) predicted habit formation.

### What is weak or contested

**The single most relevant trial to this app was null.** Babir et al. (Scand J Med Sci
Sports, 2025; NCT05574426): 77 inactive adults, mean age 54, mean VO2peak 24.5 mL/kg/min,
randomised to **bodyweight** exercise snacks or a stretching placebo. Both groups were told
to do >=3 bouts/day of <=1 min, >=3 days/week, for 12 weeks, delivered through an online
platform. Adherence was good and equal (173 +/- 74 vs 158 +/- 92 total bouts). RPE was higher
in the snacks group (3.0 +/- 0.7 vs 2.1 +/- 0.6, p < 0.001) and enjoyment was high in both
(~4.2/7). **No between-group difference in VO2peak** (relative: 0.4 mL/kg/min, 95% CI -0.6 to
1.4) and no difference in blood biomarkers. The authors' own conclusion: "Future work may
need to optimize the exercise snacks stimulus to elicit physiological changes."

The obvious reading is that RPE 3/10 bodyweight movements are not vigorous enough. Stair
sprints at RPE 8-9 work; comfortable air squats at RPE 3 did not. This should shape the
app's honest claims and its intensity coaching.

**The exercise-snacks meta-analysis is optimistic but shaky.** Wan et al. (Scand J Med Sci
Sports, 2025), 12 RCTs plus 2 non-RCTs, 483 adults: VO2max SMD 1.43 (95% CI 0.61-2.25,
p < 0.001), peak power SMD 0.68 (0.00-1.36), total cholesterol SMD -0.65 (-1.18 to -0.11),
LDL SMD -0.65 (-1.22 to -0.09). Nothing for body weight, body fat, HDL or triglycerides.
Two RCTs had high risk of bias and ten had "some concerns". An SMD of 1.43 from small
sedentary-participant trials is almost certainly inflated.

**"Greasing the groove" has no direct trial.** Tsatsouline introduced it in The Naked
Warrior (2003): frequent submaximal practice, commonly cited as about 40-60% of max reps,
4-8 sets spread through the day, 15+ minutes between sets, never to failure. Popular write-ups
claim study support, but I found no RCT of a GtG protocol itself. What exists is adjacent:
the frequency meta-analysis above, the Nosaka 3-second daily eccentric series, the failure
literature, and motor-learning distributed-practice work. The mechanism story is plausible and
the practice is low risk. Treat it as a sensible heuristic, not a proven protocol.

**Notification frequency has no established threshold.** The best data:

- Klasnja et al. (Ann Behav Med, 2019), HeartSteps micro-randomised trial, 44 adults, 6 weeks:
  a contextually tailored activity suggestion raised the following 30 min step count by 14%
  (about 35 steps) on average. On day 1 the effect was +66% (167 steps on a 253-step base),
  and it **decayed about 2% per day and was indistinguishable from zero by day 28**.
- Morrison et al. (PLOS ONE, 2017), 77 users over 2 weeks, three arms: up to 3 notifications
  per day (sensor-timed), 1 per 24 h, and 1 per 72 h. The 3/day and daily arms viewed more
  notifications (d = 0.47 and 0.44) and logged in more often than the 72 h arm, with equal
  view-and-action rates. The authors explicitly state that precise deterrence thresholds are
  unknown and that "while daily notifications are acceptable, several notifications per day
  may be unacceptable."

Nobody has tested 6-10 prompts a day for months. Design defensively.

**Guidelines context.** The 2018 Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans dropped the old
10-minute minimum bout: total moderate-to-vigorous volume is what counts, and any bout length
counts toward 150-300 min/week. Muscle-strengthening for all major muscle groups is
recommended >=2 days/week. That is what makes an app of 60-second bouts defensible at all.

## 2. Design recommendations

### Intervals and active hours

- Interval options: **60, 90, 120, 180, 240 minutes**. Do not offer anything shorter than 60
  minutes. Duran's every-30-min condition needed 5 minutes of walking, not a 45-second set,
  and 16 prompts a day is far outside anything tested for notification tolerance.
- **Default interval: 120 minutes.**
- **Default active hours: 09:00 to 19:00** (10 hours), user-editable, weekday/weekend split.
  At the 120 min default this yields 6 candidate slots.
- **Default daily cap: 5 sets.** Cap the setting itself at 8/day regardless of interval and
  active window. Below 3/day nothing in the literature applies; above 8/day nothing has been
  tested.
- **Onboarding ramp: cap the first 14 days at 3 prompts/day.** Three bouts/day is the dose
  used in Jenkins 2019, the 2024 stair-snack RCT, and Babir 2025, and 3/day is the highest
  arm ever tested in the notification-frequency trial above. Raise the cap only after the
  user completes >=70% of prompts across a 7-day window.
- Weekly target to surface in the UI: **>=4 active days/week**, from Kaushal and Rhodes'
  4-bouts-per-week-for-6-weeks habit threshold.
- Add an explicit **rest day**: one user-chosen day per week where only `mobility` and
  low-intensity `cardio` items are eligible. Prompts continue so the cue stays intact.

### Volume and rotation

- Six movement patterns: `push`, `squat`, `hinge`, `core`, `cardio`, `mobility`.
- **Maximum 2 sets of any one pattern per day**, and **minimum 3 hours between two sets of
  the same pattern**. ACSM's usual 48-hour rule between sessions for the same muscle group
  assumes a real session; these are single non-failure sets well below any fatiguing volume,
  so a same-day repeat is fine, but a third is not.
- **Never schedule the same pattern in consecutive prompts.**
- Maintain a shuffled pattern queue rather than sampling patterns independently. Build the
  day's sequence at wake time as a permutation of the pattern list, drop the excess, then
  assign exercises. This guarantees coverage instead of hoping for it.
- **Never repeat the exact same exercise id within 24 hours** while an unused eligible item
  exists in that pattern.
- Cap `cardio` at 2/day and always include >=1 `mobility` slot in any day with 4 or more
  prompts.

### Constraining the randomness

The selection function should be a filtered, weighted draw, never a uniform random pick:

1. Filter by `office_ok` if office mode is on (default on Mon-Fri 09:00-17:00).
2. Filter by `needs_floor` if the user has "no floor space" set.
3. Filter out any pattern that already hit its daily cap or its 3-hour cooldown.
4. Filter out the previous prompt's pattern.
5. Filter out any exercise done in the last 24 h if alternatives remain.
6. **First prompt of the day and any prompt within 60 minutes of the user's wake time may not
   be a high-intensity item** (`jumping_jacks`, `high_knees`, `stair_climb` at hard). Cold,
   unwarmed plyometric and near-maximal work is where the injury risk sits; warm-up before
   explosive work is a standard recommendation and there is no warm-up in this product.
   Open the day with `mobility` or an easy `squat`/`push`.
7. Last prompt of the day: weight `mobility` at 2x.
8. Among survivors, weight inversely by how recently that exercise was performed.

### Reps at each level

See the catalog below. The general shape:

- **Easy** is roughly 40-50% of a typical adult's max for that movement and should finish in
  20-40 seconds. This is the "greasing the groove" zone and is where most users should live.
- **Medium** is roughly 55-65% of max, 30-60 seconds.
- **Hard** is roughly 70-75% of max, 45-90 seconds, still with 3+ reps in reserve.
- Nothing in the catalog is prescribed to failure. Robinson et al. found strength gains flat
  across a wide range of reps-in-reserve, so there is no reason to push it and every reason
  (sweat, soreness, adherence, injury) not to.

Progression should move through **variants**, not just numbers, because that is what keeps
each set under 90 seconds. Push ladder: wall push-up -> incline push-up -> knee push-up ->
full push-up -> feet-elevated push-up. Squat ladder: sit-to-stand -> air squat -> wall sit /
squat hold -> reverse lunge. Advance a user's variant when they log 3 consecutive completions
at the `hard` number for their current variant without a reported struggle.

### Intensity coaching

This is where Babir 2025 matters. A 12-week RCT of bodyweight snacks at RPE 3.0 produced no
VO2peak change. If the app wants any cardiorespiratory claim, the cardio items have to be
prescribed at genuinely vigorous effort. Show an RPE prompt on the cardio items and target
**RPE 7-8 out of 10** ("you should not be able to hold a conversation"). Everything else in
the catalog is honestly a sedentary-break and strength-practice intervention, and the app
should say so rather than promising fitness gains it cannot support.

### Notification copy and adherence

- **Put the whole prescription in the notification.** Title: `Micro Gains`. Body:
  `20 air squats. Sit hips back, knees track toes.` The user should be able to act without
  opening the app. HeartSteps' effect came from actionable, contextual suggestions.
- Ship **two notification actions**: "Done" and "Skip". Log both. A prompt that requires
  opening the app to dismiss will get the app's notifications turned off.
- **Vary the copy.** The HeartSteps decay (about 2%/day, gone by day 28) is the strongest
  warning in this whole document: identical prompts stop working inside a month. Rotate
  phrasing, occasionally lead with a streak fact, occasionally lead with the "why".
- **Auto-throttle on non-response.** Three consecutive skips: skip the next slot silently.
  A full day of no responses: drop to 3 prompts/day the next day and send one recovery
  prompt asking whether to keep going. Never let a user hit day 5 of ignoring six daily
  pings, because the next action is disabling notifications permanently.
- **Never fire during a calendar event** if the user grants calendar access, and honour a
  Focus/Do Not Disturb check. Irrelevant timing is what turns notifications into noise.
- **Anchor to cues, not just clock times.** Lally's habit formation and Kaushal's complexity
  finding both point the same way: offer optional anchors ("after coffee", "top of the hour",
  "when I stand up from a meeting") and let the interval float +/- 10 minutes to land on a
  natural break rather than mid-sentence in a meeting.
- Surface the weekly number, not the daily one. "4 of 7 active days" beats "you missed 2 of
  6 today". Consistency is the predictor.
- Frame the goal honestly against the evidence: about **3-5 minutes a day of genuinely hard
  effort** is the dose associated with the mortality and cancer findings, and 6 x 45-second
  sets is 4.5 minutes.

### Safety notes to build in

- No max-effort plyometrics without a warm-up. Burpees, jump squats and box jumps are
  deliberately absent from the catalog for this reason; `jumping_jacks` is the ceiling.
- Plank targets top out at 60 seconds. McGill's torso endurance work treats roughly 2 minutes
  as the ceiling of clinical meaning for a front plank, and beyond about a minute the extra
  time tests tolerance rather than stability. Progress the plank by variant (long lever,
  single-leg), not by adding minutes.
- Any item with `needs_floor: true` should be suppressed by default during work hours, both
  for dignity and because a user who has to find a clean floor will just skip.

Thirty items: 6 push, 6 squat, 4 hinge, 5 core, 5 cardio, 4 mobility. Eighteen are
`office_ok`, ten need floor contact.

Deliberately excluded: burpees, jump squats, box jumps, mountain climbers, sprint intervals.
All are either high-impact without a warm-up, floor-dependent and sweaty, or both. A product
that fires unwarned prompts through the workday should not be prescribing max-effort plyos.
