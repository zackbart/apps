# "How it sees itself" claims: the 21 smaller traditions

Output: `research/claims-other.json`. Rules followed: `research/CLAIMS_BRIEF.md`, `research/REVIEW_BRIEF.md`, unslop. Each `self` sentence is 19 to 25 words, with no dashes, no semicolons, and straight quotes. One short book quote per entry at most.

## How I kept this set even with the main families

Most of these bodies came out of one of the 12 main families. I read `research/claims-main.json` (read only) so a daughter body does not get a different category from its parent unless it claims something different. Where a body split from its family over race, slavery, or sexual ethics and says its doctrine is unchanged, it takes the family's category: AME and Global Methodist follow `methodist` (renewal), Southern and National Baptists follow `baptist` (restoration), ACNA follows `anglican` (reform), Holiness follows `methodist` (renewal). Oneness Pentecostals match `pentecostal` (restoration). If the lead changes a parent category, these should move with it.

The three bodies outside the historic creeds (Latter-day Saints, Jehovah's Witnesses, Oneness Pentecostals) are described in their own words, the same way as the others. Their sentences say nothing about the Trinity debate. The map's `disputed` and `outsideNicene` fields already carry that.

## Which entries rest on tier 3 alone

- waldensians: Pelikan and Damick do not discuss the Waldensians (Pelikan only lists a medieval tract against them in vol. 3's source list). Pre-1700, but uncovered, so the church's own site carries it.
- orthodox-church-ukraine: founded 2018. Not in either book.
- global-methodist: founded 2022. Not in either book.

Thin book support, where the tradition-specific claim rests mainly on tier 3:
- african-methodist-episcopal, acna, national-baptist-convention: Damick names them or their family only. The self-description comes from their own documents.
- adventist, latter-day-saints, jehovahs-witnesses, oneness-pentecostal, charismatic, salvation-army, southern-baptist: Damick reports each one's own view. Pelikan does not cover them (no hits for Mormon, Adventist, Miller, Russell, Watch Tower, Booth, Salvation Army, Pentecostal, or charismatic in vol. 5 beyond unrelated uses).

## Entries

### waldensians: reform
- Why: The Waldensian Evangelical Church tells its story as a twelfth-century movement of fidelity to the Gospel and the poverty of the church, begun "non con l'intenzione di fondare una nuova chiesa" (not intending to found a new church). It adhered to the Reformation at the Synod of Chanforan in 1532 and constituted itself "in chiesa riformata" about twenty years later. Today it calls itself a Reformed church and belongs to the World Communion of Reformed Churches (WCC page).
- Read: chiesavaldese.org storia/valdesi page, WCC member page. Searched Pelikan vols 1 to 5 and all Damick files for Walden/Waldo/Valdes. No discussion found.
- Members disagree: the origin is a renewal movement, which fits `renewal`. I chose `reform` because the church's present self-description is a Reformed church, and it no longer sees itself as a movement inside Rome. Older Waldensian histories claimed descent from an apostolic remnant (a continuity claim). The church's current site does not make that claim, so I left it out.
- Tier 3 alone.

### moravian: reform
- Why: The Moravian Church says it began in 1457 in Kunwald, Bohemia, from Jan Hus's reform, "60 years before Martin Luther began his reformation," and calls itself "a visible part of the Body of Christ." Pelikan places the Moravian Brethren as the Hussites' spiritual descendants (5:54). Damick reports the Hus origin and the Unitas Fratrum name.
- Read: Pelikan 5:54, 5:124, 5:148 (Moravians as Spener's heirs), 4:216 (Bohemian Brethren confessions of 1535 and 1575). Damick ch4 "Moravians". moravian.org faith and history page.
- `Pelikan 5:54`, printed page 54: "spiritual descendants, the Moravian Brethren"
- Members disagree: Damick reports that in eighteenth-century America "Moravianism saw itself at first more as a movement and less as a denomination", and Pelikan 5:148 says they regarded themselves as Spener's theological heirs. Both point to the renewal side of the 1727 Herrnhut renewal. The church's present self-description rests on the 1457 Hussite reform church, so I kept `reform`.

### congregationalist: reform
- Why: Damick reports the Puritans' aim to purify the Church of England of everything that looked Roman, and the Separatists who left it. Schaff's account of the Savoy Declaration (1658), the founding Congregational confession, shows it as the Westminster Confession with a polity platform on "the Institution of Churches and the Order appointed in them by Jesus Christ", with independent local churches. Pelikan 5:285 shows the congregational free churches breaking with Anglican doctrine over the historic episcopate.
- Read: Pelikan 5:285, 4:313 (polity and the New Testament pattern, Reformed context), 5:30 (Savoy preface reaffirmed at Boston 1680). Damick ch4 "Puritans", ch3 "Anglicanism and its Heirs". CCEL Schaff, Creeds of Christendom §102. congregational.org.uk history page.
- `Damick ch4: Puritans`, quote: "they wanted anything that looked like Roman Catholicism removed from Anglicanism"
- Members disagree: the Cambridge Platform's title, "Gathered out of the Word of God", and its New Testament polity lean toward `restoration`. But Congregationalists described themselves as Reformed churches carrying the Reformation further, and Savoy kept Westminster doctrine, so `reform`.
- Not verified: I could not reach a full text of the Cambridge Platform (1648). I did not quote it.

### eastern-catholic: continuity
- Why: Vatican II's decree Orientalium Ecclesiarum says these churches keep "the tradition that has been handed down from the Apostles through the Fathers" and are "in communion with the Apostolic See of Rome." Pelikan describes Eastern churches that kept their liturgy and theology while keeping ties with Rome (5:293-294), and Vatican II's affirmation of their right to "preserve their lawful liturgical rites" (5:332).
- Read: Pelikan 5:293-294, 5:332 (index entry "Eastern Catholic churches"). Searched vol. 2 for Brest and Uniate (only a bibliography hit). Damick ch2 "SACRAMENTAL VALIDITY" mentions Eastern Catholic rites, but only as his assessment, so not cited.
- `Pelikan 5:293-294`, printed page 293: "those Eastern churches that had preserved a considerable measure of Eastern liturgy and theology"
- Note: the claim is the Catholic Church's continuity claim plus each church's own ancient rite. Some (Maronites, Italo-Albanians) say they never broke communion with Rome. Others (Ukrainian, Romanian Greek Catholics) entered it by union. The sentence fits both.

### quaker: restoration
- Why: Early Friends called their faith "primitive Christianity revived," the title of William Penn's 1696 account of Quaker faith and practice. Britain Yearly Meeting's Quaker faith & practice 19.19 gives the early Friends' conviction that "Christ has come to teach his people himself." Pelikan 5:169 records the Quaker claim to immediate revelation. Damick reports no clergy and an inward, not outward, baptism.
- Read: Pelikan 5:169. Damick ch4 "Quakers and Shakers". QF&P 19.02 and 19.19. Penn's text at inwardlight.org (Inner Light Books, a Quaker press). Baltimore Yearly Meeting draft Faith and Practice ch. 1, which uses the phrase "primitive Christianity revived".
- `Pelikan 5:169`, printed page 169: "as Swedenborg or the Quakers (and the prophet Mohammed) claimed"
- Members disagree: Damick reports that Fox "intended only to influence his fellow English Christians rather than found a new denomination," which leans toward renewal. Many liberal Friends today would not frame their faith as restored apostolic Christianity, and some are not Christocentric. The founding self-description is restoration, so I kept it.

### old-believers: continuity
- Why: Pelikan reports that the Old Believers professed complete orthodoxy, loyal to everything the holy fathers handed down "as holy and incorrupt," holding to "what the old printed books teach." They were condemned in 1666 for rejecting the corrected creed, the three-finger sign of the cross, and the corrected liturgical books (5:33).
- Read: Pelikan 5:32-34, 5:58 (Avvakum charged with tearing people from the Orthodox Church).
- `Pelikan 5:33`, printed page 33: "everything in the church, handed down to us by traditions from the holy fathers, [as] holy and incorrupt"
- Note: the Moscow Patriarchate's view (5:58) is the opposite: it saw them as schismatics. The sentence states only their own claim. No Old Believer official site in English was checked. Pelikan alone carries it.

### african-methodist-episcopal: renewal
- Why: The AME Church says it is "Methodist in terms of its basic doctrine and order of worship," holding the Apostles' Creed and the Twenty-Five Articles. It says it was "born, through adversity, of the Methodist church," and the split was "not a result of doctrinal differences" but of racial prejudice. Since it claims Methodism's own self-understanding, it takes Methodism's category.
- Read: ame-church.com "Our Beliefs". Damick ch3 "Anglicanism and its Heirs", Methodism subsection, which lists the AME among Methodist denominations.
- `Damick ch3: Anglicanism and its Heirs`, quote: "the African Methodist Episcopal Church (AME)"
- Note: `renewal` here is inherited from `methodist`. The AME makes no separate claim about the apostolic church. `other` was the alternative. I rejected it because it would suggest a theological difference that the AME itself denies.
- Book support is a listing only. The claim rests on tier 3.

### latter-day-saints: restoration
- Why: The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints teaches that after the apostles died "the fulness of the gospel was taken from the earth because of widespread apostasy," and that through Joseph Smith "the true Church of Jesus Christ is on the earth again." It teaches that John the Baptist conferred the Aaronic Priesthood and Peter, James, and John the Melchizedek Priesthood. Damick reports the same claim.
- Read: Damick ch7 "MORMONISM". churchofjesuschrist.org Topics and Questions, "Restoration of the Church" (2023).
- `Damick ch7: MORMONISM`, quote: "claims to restore the true Christian Church after centuries of apostasy"
- Note: described in the Church's own terms. The rest of Damick's section is his assessment and was not used.

### restorationist: restoration
- Why: Pelikan reports that Alexander Campbell rejected the creeds as divisive, believing the church could be reunited only by a return to precreedal "primitive Christianity," and called his followers simply "Disciples of Christ" (5:313). Damick reports the same aim. The Disciples of Christ Historical Society says Stone "called for a return to simple New Testament Christianity" and the Campbells sought "one Church of Christ upon earth."
- Read: Pelikan 5:260 (Campbell against creeds), 5:313. Damick ch5 "Restorationism". discipleshistory.org/history.
- `Pelikan 5:313`, printed page 313: "a divided church could be reunited only by a return to a precreedal "primitive Christianity,""
- Members disagree: Pelikan quotes a Disciples writer saying they "thought of themselves as a movement within the church seeking after the unity of the church", which leans toward renewal and fits today's Disciples. Damick reports that Churches of Christ "regard themselves as being identical with the New Testament Church". The movement's founding aim is restoration in both branches, so I kept it.

### holiness: renewal
- Why: Damick reports that Holiness followers believed Wesley's teaching on Christian perfection "had been eroded in the Methodist Church" and revived it through camp meetings. Pelikan shows Wesley as the main eighteenth-century spokesman for "evangelical perfection" (5:148-149), the teaching they set out to recover. The Church of the Nazarene calls itself "a Protestant Christian church in the Wesleyan-Holiness tradition", and says the movement arose in the 1830s to promote entire sanctification.
- Read: Pelikan 5:146-151. Damick ch5 "The Holiness Movement". nazarene.org "Our History".
- `Pelikan 5:148-149`, printed page 148: "Wesley was at the same time the most effective spokesman in the eighteenth century for the definition of "evangelical perfection.""
- Note: the movement began inside Methodism (Phoebe Palmer stayed Methodist) and only later formed denominations. It recovers a Wesleyan teaching, not a lost church, so `renewal` fits better than `restoration`. Pelikan covers Wesley, not the nineteenth-century movement itself.

### adventist: restoration
- Why: Seventh-day Adventist Fundamental Belief 13, "The Remnant and Its Mission", holds that the universal church is all who truly believe in Christ, "but in the last days, a time of widespread apostasy, a remnant has been called out to keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus." Damick reports that Adventists see their church as the true visible church and date the Great Apostasy to about AD 135.
- Read: Damick ch5 "Adventism". Fundamental Belief 13 at adventist.org.
- `Damick ch5: Adventism`, quote: "They also believe, however, that the Seventh-day Adventist Church is the true "visible Church,""
- Not verified directly: adventist.org blocked automated fetches (Vercel bot check, HTTP 429), so I could not load Belief 13 myself. The wording above matches several reproductions found by search, including a Southwestern Union Record article citing adventist.org. The lead should open the URL in a browser to confirm.
- Note: the node covers all Adventist churches, but the sentence describes Seventh-day Adventists, the largest. Advent Christians and others do not hold the remnant doctrine in this form.

### southern-baptist: restoration
- Why: The Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Article VI, opens: "A New Testament church of the Lord Jesus Christ is an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers", observing the two ordinances and governed "under the Lordship of Christ." Damick names the SBC and reports Baptist convictions on the local congregation and the ordinances. Same category as `baptist`.
- Read: Damick ch4 "Baptists", "The Great Apostasy". bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000 Article VI.
- `Damick ch4: Baptists`, quote: "They also believe that the local congregation is the highest authority in church government."
- Members disagree: Landmark Baptists, historically strong in the SBC, claimed an unbroken line of true churches since the apostles (Damick ch4 "The Great Apostasy"), a continuity claim. The BF&M does not make it, so restoration stands, following `baptist`.

### salvation-army: renewal
- Why: The Salvation Army's international mission statement, in its Handbook of Doctrine, says: "The Salvation Army, an international movement, is an evangelical part of the universal Christian Church." It began in 1865 as the Christian Mission in east London. Damick lists it as a Holiness denomination known for social action.
- Read: Damick ch5 "The Holiness Movement". Handbook of Doctrine (2010 text, 2025 upload), appendix "The Salvation Army in the Body of Christ". salvationarmy.org/history.
- `Damick ch5: The Holiness Movement`, quote: "Another well-known Holiness denomination is the Salvation Army"
- Note: it began as an independent mission, not inside one church, so `renewal` is not exact. But it calls itself a movement and one part of the universal Church, and it makes no claim to replace other churches. The Handbook also says it "would claim to be a church." `other` was the alternative.

### jehovahs-witnesses: restoration
- Why: jw.org says "a great apostasy began to develop, in the second and third centuries," that Jesus foretold "a restoration of true worship at the conclusion of the system of things," and that Witnesses believe "this restoration can be seen in their worldwide activities." Damick reports that they see themselves as the only Christians.
- Read: Damick ch7 "JEHOVAH'S WITNESSES". jw.org brochure "Jehovah's Witnesses: Unitedly Doing God's Will Worldwide", section "The First-Century Christian Congregation".
- `Damick ch7: JEHOVAH’S WITNESSES`, quote: "they do not want that recognition and see themselves as the only Christians"
- Note: the citation keeps Damick's curly apostrophe in the heading so it matches the chapter file exactly, as the map's existing source does.

### old-catholic: continuity
- Why: The Declaration of Utrecht (1889), which every Old Catholic bishop signs, adheres to Vincent of Lérins' rule and to the faith of the ancient Church as set out in the creeds and the councils "of the undivided Church of the first millennium". It rejects the Vatican I decrees on papal infallibility and universal jurisdiction. The WCC page says the churches "recognize apostolic succession" and the doctrine held before 1054. Pelikan shows Döllinger rejecting Vatican I as an innovation "unprecedented in 1800 years" (5:252).
- Read: Pelikan 5:251-252. Utrecht Union pages (history, Declaration text). WCC Old Catholic family page.
- `Pelikan 5:252`, printed page 252: "Dollinger felt obliged to reject its authority"
- Note: Döllinger himself never joined the Old Catholic Church. The Pelikan citation supports the stance against Vatican I that the churches took, not his membership. Neither book discusses the Old Catholic churches as such.

### national-baptist-convention: restoration
- Why: As a Baptist convention and Baptist World Alliance member, it holds the Baptist view of the church. BWA says "Believers' Baptism by immersion is the biblical way to publicly declare discipleship." Damick reports Baptist convictions on the local congregation. Same category as `baptist`.
- Read: Damick ch4 "Baptists". baptistworld.org/beliefs. A congregation's copy of the NBC USA Articles of Faith (19thstreetbc.org), whose Article XIII says "a visible church of Christ is a congregation of baptized believers". Not cited, because it is a single congregation's page.
- `Damick ch4: Baptists`, quote: "They reject sacramental theology, regarding baptism and "the Lord's Supper" (they do not usually use the term Eucharist) as "ordinances""
- Not verified: nationalbaptist.com sent every request to a captcha page, and the Internet Archive was offline. I could not read the convention's own Articles of Faith, so the sentence uses the general Baptist wording that BWA and Damick support. Damick does not discuss the NBC by name. The NBC-specific claim is effectively tier 3.

### oneness-pentecostal: restoration
- Why: The UPCI Articles of Faith (2023) make its "fundamental doctrine" "the Bible standard of full salvation": repentance, immersion "in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ for the remission of sins", and Spirit baptism with tongues, "according to the Acts of the Apostles 2:38". They teach one God who "revealed Himself as Father; through His Son, in redemption; and as the Holy Spirit". Damick reports that Oneness Pentecostals reached this view by taking Acts 2:38 literally. Matches `pentecostal`.
- Read: Damick ch6 "The Azusa Street Revival: The Spread of Pentecostalism", ch5 "The Holiness Movement". UPCI Articles of Faith PDF. upci.org "About the UPCI".
- `Damick ch6: The Azusa Street Revival: The Spread of Pentecostalism`, quote: "Some Pentecostals noticed the reference to water baptism in Jesus' name (Acts 2:38) and took it literally"
- Note: the UPCI text does not use the word "restoration". It presents its doctrine as the apostles' own pattern, and many Oneness bodies call themselves "Apostolic". Damick's label "modalism" is his assessment, and the UPCI rejects it, so it is not used.

### charismatic: renewal
- Why: Damick reports that Charismatics kept the structure of their existing churches and added Pentecostal elements "in an attempt to renew church life", and saw their renewal as a way to unite Christians. CHARIS, the Vatican-erected service for the Catholic Charismatic Renewal, states its purpose: "To spread Baptism in the Holy Spirit throughout the Church."
- Read: Damick ch6 "THE CHARISMATIC MOVEMENT", "THIRD WAVE PENTECOSTALISM" (skimmed). charis.international.
- `Damick ch6: THE CHARISMATIC MOVEMENT`, quote: "They retained the general structure of their existing churches but began to include some Pentecostal elements in an attempt to renew church life."
- Note: this is the textbook `renewal` case. Some charismatic groups later left their churches and formed independent networks. The node describes the renewal inside existing denominations.

### acna: reform
- Why: The ACNA Constitution (as amended June 2026), Article I, begins "being a part of the One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic Church of Christ". It names seven marks of "the Anglican Way": Scripture, the two sacraments, "the godly historic Episcopate as an inherent part of the apostolic faith and practice", the three creeds, the first four councils, the 1662 Prayer Book, and the Thirty-Nine Articles. The Preamble says the province formed out of "brokenness within the Anglican Communion". Same category as `anglican`.
- Read: ACNA Constitution and Canons PDF (Preamble, Articles I to III). anglicanchurch.net/welcome. Damick ch3 "Anglicanism and its Heirs".
- `Damick ch3: Anglicanism and its Heirs`, quote: "The ACNA is the most significant of these realigned groups."
- Members disagree: ACNA includes Anglo-Catholic dioceses that would stress continuity through the historic episcopate. The constitution's use of the Thirty-Nine Articles and the 1662 Prayer Book keeps it in the reformed-catholic frame, so `reform`.
- Damick only names ACNA. The self-description rests on tier 3.

### orthodox-church-ukraine: continuity
- Why: The Tomos of autocephaly (January 2019), published by the Ecumenical Patriarchate and received by the OCU, calls it the "Most Holy Church of Ukraine", now "reckoned in the body of the One, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic Church". It speaks of "the constancy of apostolic succession and canonical order", of "the historical Metropolis of Kyiv", and of the church's continuing communion with Constantinople.
- Read: ecupatria.org Tomos page. Search results on the OCU's account of the Kyiv metropolis since 988. No OCU official page was reached.
- Not verified: I could not reach the OCU's own site. The Tomos is Constantinople's text, but the OCU accepted it as its founding charter. The Moscow Patriarchate rejects this self-understanding. That dispute is already in the node's `disputed` field.
- Tier 3 alone.

### global-methodist: renewal
- Why: The Global Methodist Church says it was formed "to preserve the doctrinal integrity of the historic Christian faith," "grounded in the Wesleyan tradition." Its Articles of Faith (Article VII) say "The Church is one, holy, catholic, and apostolic," and affirm "the creedal tradition of the church universal." It claims Methodism's own place in the church, so it takes `methodist`'s category.
- Read: globalmethodist.org Articles of Faith, launch information.
- Note: the GMC was formed as a separate denomination in 2022, not as a movement inside the United Methodist Church. `renewal` is inherited from the Wesleyan family, as with the AME. `other` was the alternative.
- Not verified: the Articles of Faith web page carries a note that it is a preliminary version, not the official published text. The 2024 Book of Doctrines and Discipline PDF in the node's sources would be the firmer citation, but I did not read it.
- Tier 3 alone.
