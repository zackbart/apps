## Disagreements and resolutions

1. lutheran.summary and confession. Damick describes a two-sacrament norm and confession mainly as an early practice making a small comeback. The grid explicitly retains absolution and the Apology's broader count. [Apology XIII, paragraphs 2 to 4](https://bookofconcord.org/defense/of-the-number-and-use-of-sacraments/) permits different enumerations and explicitly names baptism, the Lord's Supper, and absolution as sacraments. Paragraphs 11 to 12 also permit sacramental language for ordination. Keep the summary, confession status varies, and ordination note. The two-sacrament count in [ELCA's explanation](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/What_is_a_sacrament_for_Lutherans.pdf) confirms variation rather than cancelling the confession.
- lutheran.confession: Retained the broader confessional account against a simplified two-sacrament description. `Damick ch3: Lutheranism`, quote: "Lutherans recognize only two sacraments, baptism and Holy Communion, though there is no official enumeration."

2. methodist.summary, baptism, and communion. Damick describes a generally symbolic Eucharist and weak baptismal sacramental theology. [This Holy Mystery](https://www.umc.org/-/media/umc-media/2013/12/19/01/24/this-holy-mystery-communion.pdf), printed pages 11 to 13, teaches real, living presence through the Spirit and grace offered independently of an individual's recognition. [By Water and the Spirit](https://www.umc.org/en/content/by-water-and-the-spirit-a-united-methodist-understanding-of-baptism) gives baptism a sacramental account of initiation, covenant, and grace. These are adopted teaching documents. Keep both sacrament statuses and the means-of-grace summary. Do not cite Damick as support for Methodist sacramental doctrine.
- methodist.communion: Retained the church's teaching rather than Damick's characterization. `Damick ch3: Anglicanism and its Heirs`, quote: "generally subscribe to a purely symbolic view of the Eucharist."

3. anabaptist.summary, baptism, and communion. Damick's broad description of Radical Reformers as treating material rites as pure symbols is narrower than the present Mennonite confession. [Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Confession-of-Faith-In-a-Mennonite-Perspective.pdf), Article 11 and its commentary, treats a sign as God's action and a human response. Article 12 says the gathered church shares in Christ's body and blood and experiences the risen Christ's presence. Keep the summary's God's work and the communion note's sharing his presence. Ordinance still describes the common classification, not an assertion that nothing spiritual occurs.
- anabaptist.summary, communion: Retained the present confession's fuller account of God's work and Christ's presence. `Damick ch4: Anti-Clericalism and Anti-Sacramentalism`, quote: "outward signs that represented God’s grace but did not actually communicate it."

4. evangelical-nondenom.communion. Damick says the revivalist acts do not convey spiritual presence. That does not cover every congregation in the grid's broad grouping. [EFCA's theological positions](https://www.efca.org/theological-positions), the ordinances section, affirms spiritual benefit in genuine faith and permits several historic evangelical accounts of Christ's presence. [Calvary Chapel Association](https://calvarycca.org/statement-of-faith/) establishes two ordinances and believers' immersion. Keep most and usually in the summary and notes, and keep the communion qualification. Replace the single congregation source with the wider evangelical body's own teaching. EFCA is a comparative example, not the definition of every independent church.
- evangelical-nondenom.communion: Retained the explicit exception for communion with Christ. `Damick ch5: Dualism`, quote: "what is happening does not convey a spiritual presence or contribute to salvation."

5. pentecostal.ordination. Damick's general claim that Pentecostals lack an established denominational clergy process conflicts with the existing ordination cell when applied to established denominations. [The IPHC Manual](https://iphc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/IPHC-Manual-April-1.pdf), Article IV, Clergy, specifies licensing, training, conference review, ordination credentials, and an ordination service. Keep status rite and the ordination note. His description better fits some independent ministries, which he also discusses. It must not erase denominational orders.
- pentecostal.ordination: Retained formal ordination against the generalization about denominational process. `Damick ch6: Touch Not the Lord’s Anointed`, quote: "There is, generally speaking, no established denominational process to produce clergy."

These five entries count disagreements about the grid's claims. The scope cautions below are not extra factual disagreements.
