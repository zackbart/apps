## Changes

### catholic.summary and catholic.communion.note

The summary stated a confessional claim without attribution. It now begins "Catholics teach that Christ instituted seven sacraments." The communion note described the change correctly but did not name transubstantiation. It now reads "Through transubstantiation, bread and wine become Christ's body and blood. Their outward appearances remain." Both statuses remain sacrament.

Pelikan distinguishes the development of sacramental enumeration from the later Catholic confession of dominical institution. The seven were not first invented at Trent. Lateran IV used transubstantiation in 1215, and Trent reaffirmed earlier teaching.

Sources: Pelikan 3:203-210 and Pelikan 4:291-293, with proof below. [Catechism 1210](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two.html) and [Catechism 1374 to 1377](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two/chapter_one/article_3/v_the_sacramental_sacrifice_thanksgiving_memorial_presence.index.html).

### baptist.communion.note

The former note mentioned spiritual nourishment but did not make the corresponding claim about presence explicit. The new note reads "Remembers Christ's death with bread and wine or juice. Some teach Christ's real spiritual presence." The status remains ordinance. The Southern Baptist memorial account and the Reformed Baptist spiritual account both use ordinance language.

Sources: [Baptist Faith and Message VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/) and [Second London Confession 30.7, published by the Reformed Baptist Network](https://reformedbaptistnetwork.com/1689lbcf/lbcfch30/). The latter explicitly teaches spiritual reception of Christ's body and blood. This is stronger evidence than the former ministry commentary.

The four parent ids represent Catholic communion and the Eastern families represented. Maronite continuous communion is expressly a Maronite claim. The 23 churches, married priests, varied Eastern rites, infant initiation, and Byzantine leavened bread are defensible.

## Pelikan citation proof and retained judgments

Each quote below is an exact short substring of the local text. Printed pages govern the citations. Scan errors are retained. A historical citation supports the named field, not every current detail elsewhere in its row.

### church-of-east.communion

Added `Pelikan 2:47-48`. Printed page 48: "is in a true sense the body of Christ through its union". The surrounding discussion relates the Eucharistic bread to Christ's heavenly body. It does not describe a modern sacramental list.

Retained all seven cells. The [2017 common statement](https://news.assyrianchurch.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Common-Statement-on-Sacramental-Life-FINAL-VERSION-18-NOV.pdf), pages 3 and 7 to 13, is decisive for present teaching. It records the 2001 synodal adoption of Abdisho's list, immediate post-baptismal signing and infant communion, Eucharistic conversion, priestly absolution, and sacramental marriage and healing. Marriage and anointing remain rite in this grid because they are outside that official counted seven. Their notes expressly preserve their sacramental character. Pelikan's older account of East Syriac Eucharistic theology does not override the modern joint confession of real and true presence.

### oriental-orthodox.summary, baptism, and communion

Added `Pelikan 2:61`. Printed page 61: "conveyed this salvation because it was trnly the body and". The next line supplies blood of Christ. The same page links baptism to adoption through the Spirit. This supports the historical account of grace and real presence, not a claim that every Oriental church already had a uniform list of seven in that period.

Retained seven sacrament statuses. Coptic and Armenian teaching support initiation with chrismation and communion, priestly absolution, marriage, holy orders, and healing. [The 2018 joint commission report](https://www.christianunity.va/content/unitacristiani/en/dialoghi/sezione-orientale/chiese-ortodosse-orientali/commissione-mista-internazionale-per-il-dialogo-teologico-tra-la/rapporti/en.html) explains the Armenian treatment of unction within initiation. Omitting a separate anointing does not warrant marking the whole family as denying its sacramental character.

### eastern-orthodox.summary and communion

Added `Pelikan 2:291-293`. Printed page 291: "this identification of seven sacraments." Printed page 292: "manner of the change from bread and wine to the body". These pages describe the adoption of the scholastic count in seventeenth-century confessions, the rejection of merely symbolic presence, use of transubstantiation, and reserve about explaining the change.

Retained seven sacrament statuses. The OCA's own account says counting seven came from the Roman Catholics and does not exhaust sacramental life. Its sacrament chapters support triple immersion, priestly chrismation, infant communion, confession, crowning, episcopal ordination, and unction. The summary's wider meaning of mystery is retained even though Pelikan also documents confessions that specify seven strictly.

### catholic.summary, baptism, confirmation, and communion

Added `Pelikan 1:163-168`. Printed page 166: "baptism, which was followed by anointing and then by". The following line supplies imposition of hands. Printed page 168: "The theologians did not have adequate concepts within". The surrounding discussion describes baptismal regeneration and the Spirit, then real Eucharistic presence before its later precise formulation.

Added `Pelikan 3:203-210`. Printed page 203: "the bread having been transubstantiated into the". Printed page 210: "because of the influence of Peter Lombard, even though". The first passage concerns Lateran IV. The second concerns adoption of the seven-sacrament list. Pages 209 to 210 distinguish earlier varying lists, the anonymous Sentences of Divinity, and Lombard's influence. Do not say Lombard invented the rites or that the count began at Trent.

Added `Pelikan 4:291-293`. Printed page 293: "seven sacraments of the church, neither more". The passage describes Trent's rejection of fewer or more than seven and its affirmation that Christ instituted them. This is the history of a Catholic doctrinal affirmation, not evidence that each rite's historical origin can be dated directly to Jesus' ministry.

Retained seven sacrament statuses. The Catechism supplies the current teaching on baptismal grace, Eastern confirmation, absolution, baptized spouses, three ordained ranks, and serious illness or age as grounds for unction. Pelikan's medieval account of extreme unction does not displace the current Catholic discipline.

### lutheran.summary, baptism, communion, and confession

Added `Pelikan 4:178-180`. Printed page 179: "ment or not, confession certainly was a means of grace,". The initial fragment is the scan's continuation of Sacrament across two lines. Retained sacrament for baptism and communion, varies for confession, and rite for the other four. ## For the lead

### 1. EUCHARIST.early.note

"Before anyone defined how" is too absolute. Pelikan 1:166-168 describes early explanations, realistic and symbolic language, and a lack of adequate concepts for the later precise doctrine. It does not say nobody offered an explanation.

Proposed note: "The early church confessed Christ's body and blood before later disputes defined the manner of presence." Keep the three early assignments. Page 168's exact proof is "The theologians did not have adequate concepts within".

### 2. EUCHARIST.mystery and its relation to transub

The assignments are defensible, but the labels can suggest Eastern churches reject the idea of conversion called transubstantiation. Pelikan 2:291-293 records Eastern adoption of that term alongside reserve about the manner of change. Page 292's exact proof is "manner of the change from bread and wine to the body".

Proposed mystery note: "Eastern churches and Old Catholics affirm Christ's body and blood. Some Eastern confessions also use transubstantiation." Keep eastern-catholic under transub. Its Catholic doctrine and Eastern liturgy are compatible. Add an explanation that these categories describe emphases and can overlap. The modern Assyrian statement also affirms that the elements become Christ's body and blood. Do not reclassify it as memorial from Pelikan's older historical account.

### 3. EUCHARIST.memorial and EUCHARIST_OF.anabaptist, baptist, evangelical-nondenom

Memorial is acceptable as a predominant account of symbolic elements. It should not imply that every assigned church denies Christ's presence. Mennonite Article 12 describes the risen Christ's presence in the gathered church. Second London 30.7 explicitly teaches spiritual presence to believers. Grace Community describes actual communion with the risen Christ despite representative elements.

Proposed name: "Memorial, with varied accounts of presence". Proposed note: "Remembrance with symbolic elements. Some churches also teach Christ's spiritual presence or presence in the gathered church." Retain the existing assignments if the qualification is visible. A dedicated varied category for these families is another possible implementation. Do not move all Baptists to Calvin's account.

Sources: [Mennonite Article 12](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/the-lords-supper/), [Second London 30.7](https://reformedbaptistnetwork.com/1689lbcf/lbcfch30/), and [Grace Community's statement](https://www.gracechurch.org/about/doctrinal-statement).

### 4. EUCHARIST.spiritual.note and EUCHARIST_OF.methodist

The Methodist assignment is defensible. "Received by faith" must describe reception, not imply that Christ becomes present only when a participant believes or recognizes him. This Holy Mystery, printed pages 11 to 12, explicitly separates Christ's promised presence from individual recognition and leaves its manner incompletely explained.

Proposed note: "Reformed and Methodist. The Spirit gives communion with Christ. Faith receives the gift, while Christ's presence precedes our response." Keep methodist in spiritual unless the product chooses to emphasize its undefined manner under varied. The present row is accurate.

Source: [This Holy Mystery](https://www.umc.org/-/media/umc-media/2013/12/19/01/24/this-holy-mystery-communion.pdf).
