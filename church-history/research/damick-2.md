## Disagreements and resolutions

Counted 18 issues below. Several concern overbroad descriptions rather than a wrong map fact. In those cases the map's narrower statement stayed.

1. ninety-five-theses.facts: Damick dates Luther's excommunication to 1520. Retained January 1521. The 1520 bull threatened excommunication, while Decet Romanum Pontificem imposed it on January 3, 1521. Primary text: https://thebookofconcord.org/sources-and-context/excommunication-of-luther/ .
2. ninety-five-theses.disputed: Damick states that Luther nailed the theses to the church door without qualification. Retained the map's caveat about the debated posting. The verified Pelikan baseline and the Lutheran-Catholic dialogue support circulation and an indulgence controversy without requiring the door story. Existing sources retained.
3. lutheran.sacraments: Damick's two-sacrament description treats confession mainly as an early practice and a small modern recovery. Retained the map's explicit sacramental status for absolution. Apology XIII.4 identifies baptism, the Lord's Supper, and absolution as sacraments. Confessional text outranks the survey. Source: https://bookofconcord.org/defense/of-the-number-and-use-of-sacraments/ .
4. lutheran.sacraments: Damick acknowledges Lutheran objections to consubstantiation but still applies the label to most Lutherans. Retained sacramental union. The Formula of Concord supplies the tradition's own term and rejects philosophical explanation as the basis of this teaching. Source: https://bookofconcord.org/solid-declaration/the-holy-supper/ .
5. reformed teaching and synod-of-dort.why: Damick sometimes describes election as salvation irrespective of the person's will. Retained the neutral grace-and-response account. Westminster 3.1 and 10.1 deny violence to the will and teach that grace makes the elect willing. Dort III/IV.16 likewise preserves and renews the will. Sources: https://www.opc.org/wcf.html and https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort . The Orthodox Communion distinction alone enters reformed.disputed.
6. westminster-confession.start and summary: Damick associates production of the Westminster Standards with Cromwell's reign and quotes the confession as 1647. Retained completion in 1646 and Scottish adoption in 1647. Parliament convened the Assembly in 1643, before Cromwell's Protectorate. Source: https://www.churchofscotland.org.uk/about-us/our-faith/book-of-confessions .
7. act-of-supremacy.facts: Damick uses divorce for Henry's request. Retained annulment. The legal issue was whether the existing marriage was valid. Source: https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/private-lives/religion/overview/reformation/ .
8. act-of-supremacy.summary and elizabethan-settlement.facts: Damick moves directly from Henry's break to Supreme Governor. Retained Supreme Head for 1534 and Supreme Governor for Elizabeth's 1559 settlement. Source: https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-7515/CBP-7515.pdf .
9. moravian.sacraments and parents: Damick places Moravians in his Radical Reformation survey and says they became similar to Anabaptists. Retained Bohemian origins, infant baptism, and Christ's presence. A chapter grouping is not a parent-child relationship or a common sacramental doctrine. The Moravian account states its own practice. Source: https://www.moravian.org/2018/06/the-moravian-church-our-faith-our-history-and-our-ministry/ .
10. baptist.summary, parents, and disputed: Damick leaves Baptist origins between Anabaptist and Puritan explanations. Retained the more specific English Separatist origin and the qualified Mennonite influence. Sharing believers' baptism does not establish a single institutional origin. The node's scholarly Baptist history remains: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7722/j.ctt81sdz .
11. baptist.sacraments: Damick broadly says Baptists reject sacramental theology. Retained the memorial description with the Reformed Baptist qualification. The 1689 confession 30.7 teaches real spiritual feeding on Christ through faith. The Baptist Faith and Message VII supplies the symbolic formulation for Southern Baptists. Sources: https://www.ccel.org/creeds/bcf/bcfc30.htm and https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/ .
12. quaker.facts and sacraments: Damick says Quaker meetings have no clergy and describes outward baptism as absent without exceptions. Retained the map's diversity and traditional qualifier. Pastoral Friends have ministers, and some evangelical Friends use outward rites. No universal no-clergy claim was added. Sources: https://www.fgcquaker.org/quakerism/branches/ and https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/12 .
13. pietism.summary: Damick labels Spener's second proposal fully congregationalist governance. Retained active lay participation and pastoral formation. Pelikan describes Pietism within the church, not as the founding of a congregational polity. The Spener proposal concerns the spiritual priesthood of believers. Scholarly cross-check: https://www.ccel.org/s/schaff/encyc09/cache/encyc09.html3 . Added only the named Orthodox concern to disputed.
14. anabaptist, schleitheim-confession, congregationalist, and baptist church life: Damick's Invisible Church section broadly subordinates visible church membership to a private relationship. Retained the map's concrete communities, membership, discipline, and congregational governance. Schleitheim II, III, and V require a disciplined baptized church with pastors. The 1689 confession 26 also requires gathered churches and officers. Primary texts: https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/an-anabaptist-confession-of-faith-the-schleitheim-articles-1527.pdf and https://www.ccel.org/creeds/bcf/bcfc26.htm .
15. anabaptist.outsideNicene and sacramental framing: Damick's Common Ground calls the Radical Reformation a rejection of the Incarnation in its implications. Retained the map's Nicene status for the Anabaptist family and its concrete water baptism, Communion, and footwashing. His theological criticism does not establish that all these churches deny the Incarnation. Damick himself acknowledges that most Radicals affirmed traditional Trinitarian and Christological doctrines. Primary baptism and church text: the Schleitheim URL in issue 14. No polemical conclusion entered the main text.
16. methodist.sacraments: Damick describes most Methodists' Communion as purely symbolic and their infant baptism as lacking strong sacramental theology. Retained both rites as means of grace and real spiritual presence. The British Methodist statement and the UMC's adopted This Holy Mystery outrank that generalization. Sources: https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/faith_and_order_statements_vol2_part1_0409_XyqQqqD.pdf and https://www.umc.org/-/media/umc-media/2013/12/19/01/24/this-holy-mystery-communion.pdf . No disputed field was used to repeat an inaccurate description of Methodist doctrine.
17. menno-simons.disputed and outsideNicene: The map had no caveat about Menno's account of Christ's flesh. Damick identifies a serious Orthodox objection. Pelikan 4, printed page 321, also distinguishes Menno's orthodox Trinitarian confession from his claim that Christ became man in Mary's womb rather than taking flesh from her. Added the specific objection to disputed. Retained the boolean because Menno affirmed Christ's divinity and the Trinity, and the map uses it for broad Nicene affiliation. This does not erase the Christological dispute or attribute it to all Mennonites. Scholarly source added: https://gameo.org/index.php?title=Christology . Search returned the relevant article text, but opening its page returned 403. Pelikan and the supplied Damick text corroborate the point.
18. westminster-confession.disputed: Damick contrasts imputed righteousness with actual transformation. A bare contrast would hide Westminster's required sanctification. Added a comparison that names the Orthodox understanding and states that Westminster distinguishes justification from sanctification while requiring holy living. Westminster 11.2 and 13.1 govern the Reformed side. Source added: https://www.opc.org/wcf.html .
