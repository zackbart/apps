## Disagreements and resolutions

1. early-church.summary and disputed, pentarchy-forming.summary. Damick's overview describes a single church with unchanged beliefs across five centers for about a millennium. The map distinguishes doctrinal development, earlier rival churches, fifth-century separations, and the later formation of the pentarchy. Retained the map's qualifications. These express historical distinctions that Damick's denominational overview compresses. The existing Pelikan citations outrank the overview. Damick is cited only for the five cities. No text change.
2. development-of-bishops.summary and disputed. Damick's introductory overview and chapter 1 locate episcopal succession directly in the apostolic church. The map describes the uneven spread of one bishop per city and explicitly states Catholic and Orthodox claims of apostolic origin. Retained both the historical account and attributed ecclesial claims. Re-read Pelikan's existing pages 98 to 99, which connect the rise of the monarchical episcopate with second-century developments. These accounts answer different questions. No text change or citation added.
3. canon-formation.summary, facts, and disputed. Damick says the canon was finalized in the late fourth century. His claim that the church could not ask what the Bible said for three centuries also goes beyond the map. The map describes gradual recognition and continued eastern debate after 397. Retained the map. Re-read the existing Pelikan page 114, which describes early scriptural authority and a fluctuating book list. Athanasius' letter supports the specific 367 list. Damick is cited for that list alone. https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm
4. marcionism.summary. Damick includes two additional writings attributed by Marcion's followers to Paul in his canon discussion. The map gives an edited Luke and ten Pauline letters. Retained the map's count. Re-read the existing Pelikan pages 79 to 81. Page 79 explicitly enumerates ten letters. Damick is cited only for the expulsion in 144 and separate church, not for his list of writings.
5. ephesus.facts and disputed, church-of-east.summary and disputed. Damick describes Nestorius as teaching two persons and refusing Theotokos without the map's qualifications. Kept the map's account of an objection to unqualified Theotokos and its warning about the Nestorian label. Re-read the existing Pelikan pages 260 to 262, which distinguish Cyril's judgment from Nestorius' intent. The Assyrian church's own confession and the 1994 joint declaration govern present teaching. The declaration affirms divinity and humanity united in one person. Added the declaration to ephesus. Did not label the modern Church of the East heretical or outside the Nicene faith. https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/1994/november/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19941111_dichiarazione-cristologica.html
6. catholic.summary, chalcedonian.disputed, great-schism-1054.summary. Damick's introduction speaks of the Roman Catholic Church's independent formation after Rome broke communion. The map treats 1054 as a marker and recognizes both churches' continuity claims. Retained that framing. Damick's fuller chapter 2 also says the separation developed over centuries. Synchronized the duplicate chalcedonian records without making the introduction's theological judgment the map's voice. The joint Catholic-Orthodox consultation supports gradual separation and limited excommunications. https://www.assemblyofbishops.org/ministries/ecumenical-and-interfaith-dialogues/orthodox-catholic/filioque-a-church-dividing-issue
7. photian-schism.facts and filioque-dispute.disputed. Damick treats the 879 to 880 council as rejecting the Filioque. The map only says it recognized Photius and describes broader disagreement over the creed. Retained the map. The 2003 joint consultation says the council reaffirmed the unaltered creed but made no statement about the Filioque's theological justification. Cited Damick only for Photius' restoration and for the Orthodox objection to the addition. https://www.assemblyofbishops.org/ministries/ecumenical-and-interfaith-dialogues/orthodox-catholic/filioque-a-church-dividing-issue
8. constantinople-iii.disputed. Damick uses Honorius' condemnation to challenge papal infallibility. Catholics accept the historical condemnation while denying that the letters were an infallible definition. Kept the existing historical facts and added both interpretations as attributed views. No claim that the council disproved or established the later Catholic dogma. https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07452b.htm
9. rise-of-islam.facts. Damick dates the beginning of Muhammad's revelations to 622. The map uses 622 for the migration from Mecca to Medina. Retained the map's specific event. The existing OCA source explicitly identifies that migration. Damick's added citation supports only gradual conversion, not his revelation date. https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/church-history/seventh-century/the-rise-of-islam
