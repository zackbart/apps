## Disagreements and resolutions

1. second-great-awakening.start and end. Damick gives roughly 1790 to 1850. The map marks 1800 to 1840 and explicitly says the revival began in the 1790s and dates vary by region. These are different conventional bounds for overlapping revivals. Kept the map's qualified dates. Damick is corroboration for conversion preaching and denominational growth, not authority for exact endpoints. Sources https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?psid=2992&smtID=2 and https://www.thearda.com/us-religion/history/timelines/entry?eid=239&etype=1.

2. african-methodist-episcopal.sacraments and methodist.steps. In his Anglican section, Damick describes most Methodists as holding purely symbolic Communion and weak baptismal theology. AME article 18 instead teaches receiving Christ spiritually through faith, and article 16 calls sacraments signs of grace. The map follows AME's own doctrine. Kept its sacramental wording and the neutral Methodist lineage sentence. The Damick citation on AME supports affiliation only. Source https://www.ame-church.com/our-church/our-beliefs/.

3. latter-day-saints.summary. Damick places the 1830 organization in Manchester. The retained LDS history places the meeting at the Whitmer house in Fayette on April 6. Kept the accurate state-level map wording and exact date. The quote proves Damick's support for 1830, not the town. Source https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teachings-joseph-smith/chapter-11?lang=eng.

4. latter-day-saints.facts[2] and disputed. Damick describes three separate gods and makes a broader polytheism judgment. The church's own statement calls the Godhead distinct beings united in purpose. Kept that self-description and the factual disagreement with Nicene doctrine. Did not import his evaluative language or claims about other LDS teachings absent from the map. Source https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/godhead?lang=eng.

5. restorationist.sacraments. Damick broadly denies real presence and says baptism accomplishes nothing in itself. Disciples explicitly affirm Christ's presence in Communion and baptism as a divine gift that incorporates believers into Christ's body. The map already distinguishes Disciples and describes the wider branches with qualifiers. Kept that wording. His citation supports the founding union, not a blanket sacramental description. Source https://disciples.org/our-identity/communion-and-baptism/.

6. holiness.summary, facts[1], and disputed. Damick describes the second blessing as granting sinlessness and contrasts it with lifelong theosis. Nazarene articles 5 and 10 distinguish deliberate sin from mistakes, a pure heart from mature character, and entire sanctification from continued growth. Kept the existing distinction about mistakes. Made the second work of grace explicit in the summary. Added a 19-word disputed sentence naming the Orthodox account while acknowledging Nazarene growth. Sources https://resources.nazarene.org/index.php/s/3qpJHL6De44ppjd/download and https://nazarene.org/what-we-believe/. The Damick quote supplies the Orthodox comparison.

7. millerite-disappointment.facts[1]. Damick attributes calculating October 22 to Miller. The map more precisely names Samuel Snow as promoter of that date and distinguishes Miller's initial 1843 or 1844 expectation. Kept the map. The added citation supports the shared failed expectation, not who first fixed that day. Source https://encyclopedia.adventist.org/article?highlight=Division&id=B8SX.

8. salvation-army.sacraments. Damick calls its policy rejection of baptism and Communion. The Army describes a sacramental life and inward communion with Christ while omitting prescribed outward rites. Kept the map's precise qualifier, no outward sacraments, and its inward grace language. Source https://www.salvationarmy.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/Handbook%2Bof%2BDoctrine.pdf, printed pages 269 to 271.

9. pentecostal.type, parents, why, and sacraments. Damick proposes Pentecostalism as a fourth kind of Christianity and describes some classical Pentecostals as requiring tongues for Christian identity. The map locates its historical roots in Protestant Holiness and Baptist circles and distinguishes Spirit baptism from water baptism. Assemblies of God truths 5, 7, and 8 distinguish salvation by faith from subsequent Spirit baptism and its evidence. Kept the map's qualified most and usually wording. Damick's fourth-kind proposal is an Orthodox comparative interpretation, not a factual reclassification. Source https://ag.org/Beliefs/Statement-of-Fundamental-truths.

10. oneness-pentecostal.disputed. Damick uses modalism as the defining label. The map names rejection of that label by Oneness believers while describing rejection of three eternal divine persons. Kept the map's respectful account. UPCI's Articles of Faith establish its own Godhead and baptism teaching. Sources https://upci.org/our-beliefs/ and https://dmc.upci.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023-UPCI-ARTICLES-OF-FAITH.pdf.

11. evangelical-nondenom.summary and facts[1]. Damick discusses nondenominational independence and megachurch growth together and generalizes about their government. The map correctly distinguishes size, affiliation, evangelical belief, and charismatic practice. Kept those distinctions. His chapter corroborates growth, not a claim that every megachurch is independent. Source https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/research/megachurch-research/.

12. fundamentalist-modernist.summary. Damick compresses fundamentalism into the Presbyterian reaction and the formation of the OPC. The map describes a wider Protestant controversy while giving OPC's 1936 founding as one outcome. Kept the broader account under Pelikan 5:326 and the Presbyterian Historical Society. The Damick citation supports reaction against Modernism only. Source https://pcusa.org/news-storytelling/blogs/historical-society-blog/journal-interview-bradley-longfield-fundamentalist-modernist-conflict.
