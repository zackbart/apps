# Independent verification of the self descriptions

Reviewed 2026-10-14. Read CLAIMS_BRIEF.md, REVIEW_BRIEF.md, unslop, both JSON files, and both writers' logs before evaluating the entries. Checked all 33 entries. Read every originally cited Pelikan page in the local text, using the printed running headers. Checked every Damick heading against the local chapter and separated reports of a tradition's beliefs from his Orthodox judgments. Opened every original web citation, with direct HTTP reads where the web reader failed.

Result: CHANGES. Corrected 20 entries, comprising 14 sentences and 12 source arrays. No ids, schema fields, or categories changed. The descriptions now use "Sees itself as" or "Holds that", agree in number, contain at most 25 words, and contain no dashes, semicolons, or curly quotes. This log contains no quotations from either copyrighted book.

A source can support a specific part of a sentence without supplying every part. I retained such citations where the passage actually reports the relevant teaching or historical self understanding. I removed citations that only name the body, describe a different person's position, or discuss a disagreement without stating the claimed church identity.

## Neutrality and open disagreements

The four ancient churches retain the same Christ-founded church and unbroken continuity frame. None is certified as the original church by the map. Other continuity entries state their own inherited faith and church identity without implying a stronger claim. Lutheran, Reformed, and Anglican reform descriptions express claimed continuity rather than a lesser kind of Christianity. Renewal and restoration describe how movements relate to earlier church life, not their truth or standing. The Latter-day Saint, Witness, and Oneness sentences use their own account of apostasy, restoration, or apostolic teaching without importing another church's verdict.

- Baptist restoration remains the least well-supported majority category. A New Testament pattern does not itself establish that other churches lost the apostolic faith. BF&M VI and the BWA beliefs also recognize the wider Christian church. Reform or other is arguable for many Baptists, while Landmark continuity is a minority account. I did not infer a lost-church claim from Damick's polemical argument that Protestants implicitly require apostasy. The parent, Southern Baptist, and National Baptist categories are retained together, but inheritance is not independent evidence.
- AME and Global Methodist renewal are inherited family choices. The bodies themselves were founded as separate denominations, and their documents support universal church membership and Methodist faith rather than a distinct claim to renew a parent from within. Other could fit under a strict reading of the brief. The sources do not clearly establish a replacement category, so neither was changed.
- Salvation Army renewal fits its revival and Holiness heritage and universal church identity. Its origin as an independent mission makes the brief's "inside another church" test imperfect. Other is arguable. Its mission statement does not settle that taxonomy.
- Quaker restoration is the founding Christian account established by Penn. Liberal and nontheist Friends need not describe themselves as restored Christianity or use Christ's guidance language. Christian pastoral Friends also differ from the silent-meeting model. The revision removes the misleading rites generalization but cannot make one Christian self account binding on every Friend.
- Evangelical nondenominational other is reasonable for the representative beliefs cited, but no single confession binds this whole family. Particular churches make explicit New Testament restoration claims. Neither NAE nor Lausanne proves what every independent church says.
- Moravian reform reflects its Hussite church identity. Its eighteenth-century renewal gives a defensible renewal alternative. Restorationist covers the founding Stone and Campbell aim and the Churches of Christ, while today's Disciples often emphasize unity within the wider church.
- Adventist restoration and the sentence describe the largest body's teaching. The explicit scope wording prevents it being read as a unanimous Adventist confession. The single family category remains an aggregation choice.
- Anglican and ACNA reform are defensible in the reformed catholic tradition, while Anglo-Catholic members stress continuity. Waldensian reform is supported by its present church identity even though its original movement was a Gospel renewal. Anabaptist restoration is directly supported historically, while current Mennonites also use renewal language. These flagged choices did not warrant category changes.
- Oneness restoration is supported by the apostolic pattern its sources claim, but the cited UPCI document calls it the Bible standard rather than naming the historical category "restoration". Retained as an arguable interpretation, not merely because its parent has that label.
- National Baptist Convention's own Articles of Faith remain unverified because its site redirects to a CAPTCHA. BWA membership and general Baptist sources support the family description, but cannot prove a convention-specific restoration claim.
